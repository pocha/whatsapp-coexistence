How to Get WhatsApp API for Free

Make sure you've already created a Business Portfolio before starting this. Even if you do not have a registered business, you need to create a business portfolio, and it will stay "unverified", which is ok.

  1. Go to developers.facebook.com/apps and create a new App (Meta's general Create an App doc covers this step for any app type, if you want more detail).
  2. When asked what you want your app to do, choose the "Connect with customers through WhatsApp" use case (not "Create an app without a use case" or "Other") — this adds the WhatsApp product, described in Meta's WhatsApp Business Accounts doc, along with the whatsapp_business_messaging / whatsapp_business_management permissions, automatically.
  1. On the Business step, select the Business Portfolio you created earlier (not your personal profile) and continue.
  1. On the Requirements step you'll likely see "No requirements identified." That's expected, not an error — using the API for your own number (rather than building a platform for other businesses) doesn't require anything extra here. Click Next.

The final overview — confirm everything looks right, then finish creating the app. (The app name shown here, "WA Coexistence", is ours — name yours whatever you like.)

Set up the WhatsApp use case

Once the app is created you land on its dashboard. Your app can already use the WhatsApp API under Standard Access, which is all a business needs on its own number. Click Customize the Connect with customers through WhatsApp use case.

Your checklist may show fewer items than this one.

  1. Under Basic setup, go to Step 2. Production setup. Step 1. Try it out is only for first tests with Meta's test number.
  2. Complete Register your WhatsApp phone number and Add payment to send business-initiated messages. The token step needs a registered number.

Get an access token and send a message

To send messages you need an access token. You don't need the App ID or App Secret for this.

  1. Open Send message, and under Step 1: Generate token click Generate token. Meta describes this as a permanent access token for your WhatsApp Business account. Copy it and store it somewhere safe.
  2. Under Step 2: Send a message from your registered number, choose your number, enter a recipient and a message, and send it. The recipient should receive it in WhatsApp.

If that doesn't work: create a system user token

Meta links to this manual route from the same screen when token generation fails.

  1. Open Business Settings at business.facebook.com/latest/settings and choose the right portfolio from the switcher at the top left. Then go to Users → System users and click + Add. Name it, and pick the Admin or Employee role.
  2. Click Assign assets and give the system user Manage app control over your app. Also assign your WhatsApp account so the token can reach it.
  3. Click Generate token, select your app, and choose an expiration.
  4. Select the permissions business_management, whatsapp_business_management and whatsapp_business_messaging, then copy the token. Store it somewhere safe, because Meta won't show it again.

Source: Access tokens — Meta for Developers. Exact button names can change as Meta updates the dashboard.

Receive incoming messages: set up the webhook

WhatsApp delivers messages people send to your number by calling a URL you provide, so your app needs to be reachable over public HTTPS.

  1. In Step 2. Production setup, open Configure Webhooks. Enter your app's URL as the Callback URL, and a Verify token: any string you choose, which your app must check when Meta calls it.

Don't have an app yet? Set up our test chatbot. It runs on your computer, gives you a public HTTPS URL to use as the Callback URL, and shows incoming messages in its logs. Use the same verify token in its .env and here.

Use your own URL and verify token here.

  1. Click Verify and save. Meta sends your URL a verification request, and your app must answer it correctly.
  2. You can see your app's permissions under Permissions and features (under Other tools in the left menu). The WhatsApp use case adds them for you: whatsapp_business_messaging sends messages, and whatsapp_business_management is for managing templates and account settings through the API. On your own number they work at Standard Access, so there is nothing to add and no app review. Don't request Advanced Access.
  1. Back in Configure Webhooks, scroll to the Webhook fields list. Make sure messages is set to Subscribed. Meta subscribes the common fields for you, but it is worth checking, since without it you won't receive incoming messages.
  1. Send a WhatsApp message to your number from another phone. It reaches your app. With the test chatbot, you'll see it in its logs.

The test chatbot's logs: the incoming message event, then its reply going out through the relay.

⚠ NO COEXISTENCE ON THIS ROUTE

Getting API access this way does not give you coexistence. A number registered on the Cloud API through your own app can't also be used with the WhatsApp Business app. To keep using the Business app on the same number, use coexistence through us instead. Meta's Business app onboarding doc explains the options.
